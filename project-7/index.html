01 / RESEARCH

UNDERSTANDING THE HUMAN SIGNAL

Project 7 investigates whether measurable patterns within physiological signals can be processed, modeled, and classified through computational methods.

The research builds upon previous experimental experience with EKG signal acquisition, R-R interval analysis, heart-rate measurement, and statistical evaluation.

The objective is to develop and evaluate methods for transforming physiological signals into meaningful computational information.

02 / SIGNAL CLASSIFICATION
REFERENCE PATTERNS / EDUCATIONAL RESEARCH

WHEN THE SIGNAL CHANGES

Heart rate is only one part of a physiological signal. Project 7 is interested in the relationship between rate, rhythm, R-R intervals, waveform features, and the conditions in which those signals were recorded.

REF 01 REFERENCE

NORMAL SINUS RHYTHM

A reference pattern with organized electrical activity and repeating R-R intervals. Resting heart rate in typical adults is commonly discussed in the range of 60–100 BPM, but context matters.

RATE~60–100 BPM* R-RREGULAR PATTERN
REF 02 RATE CHANGE

BRADYCARDIA

A resting heart rate below 60 BPM is generally described as bradycardia. A lower rate is not automatically a disease; fitness, medication, sleep, and individual physiology can matter.

RATE<60 BPM* QUESTIONWHY IS RATE LOWER?
REF 03 RATE CHANGE

TACHYCARDIA

A resting heart rate above 100 BPM is generally described as tachycardia. Exercise, stress, fever, medications, and other conditions can increase heart rate, so rate alone does not establish an abnormal condition.

RATE>100 BPM* QUESTIONWHAT CONTEXT?

THE SIGNAL RESPONDED.

In the archived measurements, the post-exercise R-R intervals became shorter and the calculated heart rate increased. That is exactly the type of measurable physiological response that gives Project 7 a starting point: a raw signal can become a quantitative observation when its features are detected consistently.

RESEARCH BOUNDARY

Project 7 is an experimental research initiative. It does not diagnose heart disease, heart failure, arrhythmia, or other medical conditions. Any future clinical application would require appropriate datasets, clinical validation, ethics and privacy review, and applicable regulatory pathways.
